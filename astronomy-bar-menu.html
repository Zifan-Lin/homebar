Horizon

A cocktail menu for those who look up

Every drink is named for a mission, instrument, or phenomenon from the astronomical literature. The acronyms are real. The expansions are … negotiable.

Missions & Surveys
TESS
Acronym
TEquila, Sweet & Sour // Transiting Exoplanet Survey Satellite
née Margarita
TESS hunts exoplanets by monitoring stellar brightness for the faint dimming of a transit. This drink operates on an identical principle — it dims the observer, gradually and reproducibly, on a well-characterised timescale. The acronym reverse-engineers itself with embarrassing elegance: tequila contributes both the T and the E; lime does the rest. Salt rim mandatory.
2 oz blanco tequila ¾ oz triple sec ¾ oz fresh lime salted rim
GMT
Acronym
Gin, Mixer (tonic), Twist of lime // Giant Magellan Telescope
née Gin & Tonic
The Giant Magellan Telescope will be the largest optical telescope on Earth when completed at Las Campanas Observatory, Chile — seven 8.4-metre mirrors resolving angular detail ten times finer than Hubble. This drink resolves nothing. It requires almost no assembly and tastes exceptional. A monument to elegant minimalism.
2 oz London dry gin 4 oz tonic water lime wedge
ELT
Acronym
Eau-de-vie, Lemon, Triple sec (Cointreau) // Extremely Large Telescope, ESO
née Sidecar
ESO's Extremely Large Telescope — a 39-metre primary on Cerro Armazones, Chile — will be, by some margin, the largest optical/near-IR telescope ever constructed. Three ingredients, precise ratio, served in a sugar-rimmed coupe. Not the tallest drink at the bar. Extremely the best drink at the bar.
1½ oz cognac ¾ oz Cointreau ¾ oz lemon juice sugar rim
WISE
Acronym
Whiskey, lemon juIce, Simple syrup, lEmon // Wide-field Infrared Survey Explorer, NASA
née Whiskey Sour
NASA's Wide-field Infrared Survey Explorer mapped the entire sky in mid-infrared, cataloguing hundreds of millions of stars, brown dwarfs, and galaxies. Note: the I and E are borrowed from within "lemon juice" — a flagrant violation of standard acronym convention, and therefore entirely consistent with astronomical tradition.
2 oz bourbon ¾ oz lemon juice ½ oz simple syrup no egg white
JUICE
Acronym
Juniper gin, lime jUIce, Chilled, servEd up // JUpiter ICy moons Explorer, ESA
née Gimlet
ESA's JUICE is currently en route to the Jovian system, targeting Ganymede, Callisto, and Europa — moons believed to harbour vast subsurface liquid water oceans. The Gimlet is gin plus lime juice. The drink is, literally, juice. The astronomers who named this mission knew exactly what they were doing. The recursion is not accidental. It is the entire point.
2 oz gin ¾ oz fresh lime juice ¾ oz simple syrup served up
Instruments & Spectrographs
WINERED
Acronym
Warm INfrared Echelle spectrograph to Realize Dispersion // Magellan/Clay Telescope, Las Campanas
née New York Sour
WINERED is a high-resolution near-infrared spectrograph on the 6.5-metre Magellan Clay telescope. Its name contains the letters W-I-N-E-R-E-D, in that order. A New York Sour is a whiskey sour finished with a red wine float. The drink is wine, plus red. The name is WINERED. No further justification is required, available, or possible.
2 oz bourbon ¾ oz lemon juice ½ oz simple syrup red wine float
V‑band
Notation
Johnson–Cousins V filter  ·  λeff = 551 nm  ·  green // standard visual photometric passband
née Midori Southside
The V-band is the green channel of the canonical UBVRI photometric system, centred at 551 nm and designed to approximate the peak sensitivity of the dark-adapted human eye. Midori is Japanese for green. Emission nebulae almost never look green — they skip straight from blue to red. This drink is aggressively, defiantly green. A photometric anomaly. Order it anyway.
1½ oz gin 1 oz Midori ¾ oz lime juice ½ oz simple syrup 8 mint leaves
Stellar Physics
OBAFGKM
Acronym
Old fashioned, Bourbon, Angostura bitters, old Fashioned, oranGe peel, whisKey, siMple syrup // Harvard stellar spectral classification sequence
née Old Fashioned
O B A F G K M — the Harvard spectral sequence, from O-type blue supergiants at ~50,000 K down to M-type red dwarfs at ~3,000 K, traditionally memorised as "Oh Be A Fine Girl, Kiss Me." "Old Fashioned" appears twice — once for O, once for F — a recursive self-reference that is either a logical flaw or a deliberate artistic choice. Astronomers will know which.
2 oz bourbon 2 dashes Angostura 1 tsp simple syrup orange peel large ice cube
Served neat = O-type (50,000 K).    On the rocks = M-type (3,000 K).    Choose your temperature.
Phenomena
Vertical Transport
Abstract
Convective energy transport in stellar interiors // buoyancy-driven upward motion of hot plasma
née French 75
In the convective zones of solar-type stars, columns of hot plasma rise buoyantly, deposit their energy at the surface, cool, and descend — a continuous cycle of vertical transport that carries fusion energy outward over thousands of years. The bubbles in this glass do the same thing, on a somewhat compressed timescale. The gin is the energy source. The sparkling wine is the convective envelope.
1 oz gin ½ oz lemon juice ½ oz simple syrup sparkling wine to top lemon twist
CME
Acronym
Corona, Mainly Enjoyed // Coronal Mass Ejection
née Corona (beer)
A Coronal Mass Ejection is a sudden eruption of magnetised plasma from the Sun's outer atmosphere, capable of disrupting satellites and power grids across the planet. This is a Corona with a lime wedge. The solar corona is also ~200× hotter than the photosphere beneath it — the Coronal Heating Problem, unsolved since 1939. This drink will not help solve it. Order it anyway.
1× Corona Extra lime wedge, squeezed in
Tcorona ~ 1–3 MK  ·  Tsurface ~ 5,778 K  ·  heating mechanism: unknown  ·  drink: refreshing
